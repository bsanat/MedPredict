# U03 — Dana Ilyasova: synthetic interview

Date: 16 October 2026 (Asia/Almaty).

All people, answers and quotes in this document are fictional **AI-generated simulation**. This is not a real interview or verified research. The dialogue is an exploratory writing exercise; human participant research and clinician/expert research are still needed. Interviews cannot evaluate model accuracy, performance or Python–Java parity. No screenshot or live interface was supplied, so no observed usability is established.

MedPredict is an educational diploma project at Astana IT University by Sanat Bogenbayev, Ali Baymen and Oralkhan Seilkhan. M. K. Turarova is named as advisor in the attachment, but advisor confirmation remains outstanding in the repository. The React/TypeScript/Vite frontend is implemented; Java/Spring Boot and ML work are ongoing; ONNX is proposed. MedPredict is not intended as a medical device and provides no diagnosis, treatment advice or basis for clinical decisions. No clinical certification is established here.

Fictional participant: Dana Ilyasova, 31, single, a frontend developer with a bachelor’s degree in computer science, living in Astana, Kazakhstan. Her income is upper-middle relative to the invented sample. Assigned attitudes: health consciousness 6/10, openness to health technology 9/10, medical AI trust 5/10 and privacy sensitivity 7/10. These fictional scores are persona constraints, not measurements or market evidence.

Sources: [own revised profile, U03](../profiles-v2.md#u03--dana-ilyasova-ai-generated-simulation); [interview script](../interview_script.md).

## Q01

**Interviewer (AI-generated simulation):** This fictional exercise explores existing practices and a later educational concept. You may skip any question; please do not provide personal diagnoses or actual readings. Tell me about the last time you needed to understand a health-related number or a research result; what were you trying to do?

**U03 (AI-generated simulation):** After a routine check-up, I wanted to understand why two items in the report used unfamiliar units. I was preparing questions for my next appointment, not trying to diagnose myself. Being a frontend developer helps me read documentation, but it does not give me clinical training.

## Q02

**Interviewer (AI-generated simulation):** Which digital or non-digital tools, if any, do you use for that task, and how do they fit into your routine?

**U03 (AI-generated simulation):** I usually open reports on my laptop and keep occasional notes. My exercise watch gives me activity context, but I do not automatically transfer its data to experimental services. I browse on my iPhone when away from home, and search health topics a few times a month.

## Q03

**Interviewer (AI-generated simulation):** Walk me through how you currently keep track of the information and decide what it means.

**U03 (AI-generated simulation):** I put documents in a private cloud folder with device access controls, then note dates, units and questions separately. With an unexpected reading, I first check whether the measurement process made sense and contact a professional if concerned. I rely on clinician explanations for meaning, rather than treating my notes as an assessment.

## Q04

**Interviewer (AI-generated simulation):** Where, if anywhere, does that process become difficult, and what happened in a recent example?

**U03 (AI-generated simulation):** The difficult part is distinguishing a measured value from something estimated, especially when a report assumes I understand the terminology. Recently I had to reopen an older document to check whether the units matched. That was inconvenient, but I do not have a daily monitoring problem that demands another app.

## Q05

**Interviewer (AI-generated simulation):** What alternatives have you tried, and what led you to keep, combine or stop using them?

**U03 (AI-generated simulation):** I have tried generic AI dashboards out of curiosity. They made patterns look convincing without always explaining the source or limits, so I kept my notes and professional conversations instead. Technical documentation and product demos help me understand software; they cannot establish whether its health interpretation is appropriate for me.

## Q06

**Interviewer (AI-generated simulation):** MedPredict is an educational prototype for demonstrating an indicator-prediction workflow. Its implemented frontend uses React, TypeScript and Vite. Backend and machine-learning integration are under development. The current demo returns a fixed example, not a calculation based on your input. This tool does not provide a diagnosis or treatment advice and is not for clinical decisions. No model-accuracy claim is supplied.

After hearing the concept card, how would you describe what MedPredict can and cannot tell you, and what would you do with its output?

**U03 (AI-generated simulation):** It can illustrate a workflow, but the current number tells me nothing about my health or the values I enter. I would experiment with fabricated inputs to understand the explanation and boundaries. I am curious about what is implemented, but would not take this output into a personal health decision.

## Q07

**Interviewer (AI-generated simulation):** If two different fabricated input sets both produce 134.2 mmHg in this demo, what would that mean to you, and what questions would remain?

**U03 (AI-generated simulation):** That is consistent with the fixed example described, not evidence that the inputs have equivalent medical meaning. I would ask whether the explanation keeps that distinction visible beside the number and in exports. I would still not know how any future model behaves, or whether it has been evaluated appropriately.

## Q08

**Interviewer (AI-generated simulation):** What evidence, if any, would you need before using a model-generated result for your intended task, and where would professional judgment fit?

**U03 (AI-generated simulation):** For learning, I need understandable definitions, units, provenance and an explanation of what an estimate represents. Before considering any future model result relevant to my reports, I would need documented intended use and independent evaluation appropriate to it. Professional judgment remains central to health decisions; technical polish would not change that.

## Q09

**Interviewer (AI-generated simulation):** In the current demo, indicators are not sent over the network. Future real mode would send supplied values to the configured server. Frontend memory-only handling does not establish backend retention or security.

What would you want to know about data handling before deciding whether to enter fabricated values or your own information in the demo and future real-mode contexts described?

**U03 (AI-generated simulation):** Fabricated values are acceptable for this demo. For personal information, I need a separate reason to disclose it, plus a clear account of the server operator, encryption, retention, logs, deletion and any onward sharing. Browser memory alone does not answer those questions, and I would not connect my watch automatically.

## Q10

**Interviewer (AI-generated simulation):** This is explicitly a hypothetical counterfactual about unencrypted transport, not a claim that MedPredict currently transmits plaintext health data. Do not enter actual personal measurements for this scenario.

Imagine a different setup that transmits entered health information without transport encryption; how would that affect your decision, and why?

**U03 (AI-generated simulation):** I would refuse to send personal health information in that hypothetical setup. I understand enough browser debugging to recognize that transport protection matters, but checking requests would not prove the whole service secure. Fake inputs could support a reversible experiment; my own records would remain outside it until the conditions were justified.

## Q11

**Interviewer (AI-generated simulation):** Looking only at the described inputs—age, sex, BMI, total cholesterol and glucose—how would you obtain and enter them for your intended task, and what might make you stop?

**U03 (AI-generated simulation):** I would use a supplied fictional example and type its values manually, checking the stated units and how BMI is defined. My own routine reports may not contain every required item together. Missing values, unclear units or pressure to guess would make me stop; I would not arrange tests just to complete this demonstration.

## Q12

**Interviewer (AI-generated simulation):** Where, if anywhere, could this educational prototype fit into a task you actually have, and what would make it irrelevant?

**U03 (AI-generated simulation):** It might help me frame a better question about estimates versus measurements before an appointment, if the educational explanation is useful. That is a small, occasional task for me as an adult reading my own reports. If it only displays a fixed number, it adds little to my notes and cannot support ongoing monitoring.

## Q13

**Interviewer (AI-generated simulation):** What information, if any, would you need to keep or share after that task, and how would a downloaded report help or hinder?

**U03 (AI-generated simulation):** I would keep the definitions or questions I learned, not store a demo result as part of my medical history. A downloaded report could be useful only if its fictional inputs, fixed-example status and limitations remain obvious. Otherwise it could confuse my private record folder with something that looks like a measured clinical report.

## Q14

**Interviewer (AI-generated simulation):** What would make the described experience understandable and usable on your usual device and in your preferred language, and what would you need to try to judge it?

**U03 (AI-generated simulation):** I would prefer clear Russian wording, familiar units and explanations close to the fields. On my laptop I would try keyboard navigation, visible focus and error associations; on my iPhone I would check readability and entry. Those are things I know from frontend work, but I have not tried this interface and cannot judge it yet.

## Q15

**Interviewer (AI-generated simulation):** What concerns or missing information would make you decline the prototype, even if it worked as described?

**U03 (AI-generated simulation):** I would decline if the language implied a personal prognosis, if the fixed example could be mistaken for a measurement, or if personal data were requested without a useful reason. I enjoy non-certified experiments on fake data with visible boundaries. My tolerance for reversible software experiments does not extend to medical consequences.
