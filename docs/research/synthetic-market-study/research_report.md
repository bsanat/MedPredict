These are not verified user interviews, validated market findings or clinical evidence.** No real participant was recruited or interviewed. ## Executive interpretation

The defensible output is a set of questions for the diploma team, not a product or market decision. The generated corpus suggests exploring a narrow educational value proposition: help a student/researcher discuss the relationship between fabricated input, a clearly labeled output and the limits of available evidence. It also generates counterexamples: adults interested in their indicators may need measured records or professional explanation that today’s fixed demo does not provide. This interpretation remains hypothetical and partly reflects the supplied persona roles.

Preserve the completed frontend and ongoing backend/ML work. First collect real, consented discussion evidence and have the supervisor review the task. Separately evaluate model accuracy and system behavior when agreed data and components exist. Do not implement features, redesign the UI or claim patient value from the simulations.

## Two independent technical research objectives

1. **T01 Prediction accuracy:** compare the neural network with training-mean and linear-regression baselines using MAE and RMSE on the same held-out data, with person/time leakage prevention and predeclared analysis. All results remain **not measured**.
2. **T02 System performance:** measure actual API response times with recorded environment/attempts/errors and compare Python versus Java predictions for the same frozen artifact, preprocessing and declared numerical tolerances. All results remain **not measured**.

These objectives have a dedicated [experimental_evaluation_plan.md](experimental_evaluation_plan.md). They were added directly by the user and are not evaluated or justified as successful by the interviews. Runtime equality does not imply model accuracy; low error does not establish clinical usefulness; a fast API does not establish demand.

## Provenance, limitations and project preservation

[source register](sources.md) separates local implementation evidence, limited primary-source context, generated statements and proposals. Two external language-model studies were checked at abstract/metadata level only; their human-data-grounded settings do not validate wholly invented MedPredict personas. No completed literature review, clinical consultation, real-user study or market-size analysis is claimed.

The script supplies strong non-clinical and privacy framing, and personas are created from assumptions. Shared-model agreement, coherent prose and explicit refusal of an obvious unsafe scenario may reflect prompting more than plausible real behavior. Role-based groupings are descriptive and not validated customer segments. Only actual user observations and separately executed experiments can change the evidence status.
