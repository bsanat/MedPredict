# Interview script — AI-generated research instrument

Date: 16 October 2026 (Asia/Almaty). This script is generated for exploratory simulation and a later human pilot. It is not evidence that any interview occurred. Use only the revised [profiles-v2.md](profiles-v2.md) for simulations. Every simulated interviewer utterance, participant answer and quotation must be labeled AI-generated simulation.

MedPredict is an educational diploma project at Astana IT University by Sanat Bogenbayev, Ali Baymen and Oralkhan Seilkhan. The attachment names M. K. Turarova as advisor; repository confirmation remains outstanding. React/TypeScript/Vite frontend exists; Java/Spring Boot and ML work are ongoing; ONNX is proposed. MedPredict is not intended as a medical device or for diagnosis, treatment or clinical decisions; no clinical certification is established here. Simulations cannot replace real users, clinicians or ethics/regulatory expertise.

## Administration

Begin with an open explanation of research participation and an invitation to skip questions. For a human pilot, obtain consent separately, avoid personal diagnoses/readings and use fabricated examples. Do not reveal the product, neural-network framing or the team’s preferred answers during Q01–Q05. Ask about recent practices before presenting the concept. Do not assume a problem exists. Seek reasons to reject the tool as readily as reasons to adopt it; use neutral prompts such as “What makes you say that?” without correcting the answer before initial comprehension is recorded.

At Q06 only, read this neutral concept card: “MedPredict is an educational prototype for demonstrating an indicator-prediction workflow. Its implemented frontend uses React, TypeScript and Vite. Backend and machine-learning integration are under development. The current demo returns a fixed example, not a calculation based on your input. This tool does not provide a diagnosis or treatment advice and is not for clinical decisions. No model-accuracy claim is supplied.” This conveys necessary limitations without asserting usefulness, safety or security. No screenshot or live interface is supplied in the simulations, so they cannot establish observed usability.

At Q09, distinguish two described contexts: current demo does not send indicators over the network; future real mode would send supplied values to the configured server. Frontend memory-only handling does not establish backend retention or security. Q10 is a hypothetical counterfactual, not a claim that MedPredict currently transmits plaintext health data. Do not collect any actual personal measurements to test that scenario.

## Questions — exactly 17 core turns

### Warm-up / context — 2 questions

1. **Q01:** Tell me about the last time you needed to understand a health-related number or a research result; what were you trying to do?
2. **Q02:** Which digital or non-digital tools, if any, do you use for that task, and how do they fit into your routine?

### Organic problem and existing alternatives — 3 questions

3. **Q03:** Walk me through how you currently keep track of the information and decide what it means.
4. **Q04:** Where, if anywhere, does that process become difficult, and what happened in a recent example?
5. **Q05:** What alternatives have you tried, and what led you to keep, combine or stop using them?

### Riskiest assumptions — 10 open-ended questions

6. **Q06 (A02):** After hearing the concept card, how would you describe what MedPredict can and cannot tell you, and what would you do with its output?
7. **Q07 (A02):** If two different fabricated input sets both produce 134.2 mmHg in this demo, what would that mean to you, and what questions would remain?
8. **Q08 (A05):** What evidence, if any, would you need before using a model-generated result for your intended task, and where would professional judgment fit?
9. **Q09 (A03):** What would you want to know about data handling before deciding whether to enter fabricated values or your own information in the demo and future real-mode contexts described?
10. **Q10 (A03):** Imagine a different setup that transmits entered health information without transport encryption; how would that affect your decision, and why?
11. **Q11 (A04):** Looking only at the described inputs—age, sex, BMI, total cholesterol and glucose—how would you obtain and enter them for your intended task, and what might make you stop?
12. **Q12 (A01):** Where, if anywhere, could this educational prototype fit into a task you actually have, and what would make it irrelevant?
13. **Q13 (A06):** What information, if any, would you need to keep or share after that task, and how would a downloaded report help or hinder?
14. **Q14 (A07):** What would make the described experience understandable and usable on your usual device and in your preferred language, and what would you need to try to judge it?
15. **Q15 (A01/A02/A05):** What concerns or missing information would make you decline the prototype, even if it worked as described?

### Value and willingness to pay — 2 questions

16. **Q16 (A08):** What, if anything, do you currently spend to accomplish the task you described, and which costs relate to tools rather than care, devices or research work?
17. **Q17 (A08):** For the educational prototype as described today, what, if anything, would you be willing to pay, on what basis—free access, subscription, per use or another arrangement—and what would determine that choice?

A response of zero, unknown, no relevant need, or no authority to buy is valid. Do not suggest a price or infer a subscription budget from general health spending. Any hypothetical future validated service is a different offering and must be excluded from current-prototype WTP totals.

## Simulation recording contract

One independent persona agent per U01–U10. Each sees only its own revised 30-field profile and this neutral script, not other personas or transcripts. Record every core interviewer turn and its answer, with stable Q01–Q17 headings. An agent writes one complete fictional dialogue; this is not an adaptive human conversation or a recording. Do not force enthusiasm, skepticism or zero WTP beyond the persona constraints. Additional neutral follow-ups may clarify a contradiction, but never replace a core question. No actual medical values or instructions for treatment are requested. Keep the declared educational scope intact and do not invent actual browser observations, dataset evaluation, certification or security audits.
